# The ask

The brand's twist. A person in their own day. One bar of brand glass with their own words
typed into it. One line back, beneath, over the picture. That is the whole product in a
second: not an app shown, a sentence answered.

**The bar.** Brand glass, the same material as every panel, white at 72 percent with the lens
map and an 18px backdrop blur (the film bakes it at 9px over the plate). 1320 by 144px on a
1920 frame, centred, its centre at 56 percent of frame height; `radius-ask`; padding 0 28 0
32; gap 24. Left: the mark on a 40px blue square at `radius-tile`, then the name at 22px in
`muted`. Middle: the ask at 48px, weight 500, `ink`, tracking -0.02em, a solid caret. Right: a
68px blue circle with a white up arrow at 2.4px stroke. Nothing else in the bar: no
microphone, no mode switch, no attachments, no placeholder text.

**The answer.** 108px beneath the bar's centre, centred on the frame, 68px, weight 500, white
over the picture with a soft shadow, never in a box. Two short sentences: what happened, and
what that means for them. The second sentence in `blue-light`. Under twelve words.

**Motion.** The bar fades and rises over six frames. The ask types at the film's rate,
resolving by 55 percent of the shot, step clamped 0.6 to 1.25 frames per character. The send
dips once when the ask completes. The answer rises over nine frames, four frames after the
send. Nothing loops.

**Copy.** The ask is the person's own words, lower-case verbs, no product noun, no question
mark unless they asked one. The answer never says what it will do; it says what it did. Pairs
on the film: "What changed while I was asleep?" / "Three things. Already handled." · "Is Dad's
check-up still Thursday?" / "Thursday, 9:30. Leo's driving him." · "Anything expiring soon?" /
"The passport. Renewal drafted." · "Move the client call to 11" / "Moved. They already know." ·
"Move the flight to Saturday" / "Checked. £38 more, same bags." · "Share the plan with the
others" / "Shared. One copy each."

**Placement.** The bar may cross the person's body below the shoulders, because it is theirs;
it never crosses a face, and the answer never sits on one. The person stays in the right two
thirds where possible; the bar is symmetric so the frame reads centred either way.

**On the web and in ads.** The same bar scales with the frame: width 68.75 percent of the
frame and 7.5 percent of the width tall, the ask at 2.5 percent of the width, the answer at
3.54 percent.

**Never.** A competitor's controls (no plus, no mode pill, no speak button), a tagline in the
answer's place, a second bar, a boxed answer.

**Why it is ours.** The pattern others use puts a product name above and a slogan beneath a
prompt box. Ours puts the person's sentence in the bar and the product's sentence beneath it,
in the film's own answer type, in the one brand blue. The mark chip and the name are the only
branding. The picture is real, the glass is the brand's material, the answer is the proof.
