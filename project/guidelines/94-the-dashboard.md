# The dashboard

The product's home surface, and the film's second proof. It looks like a real dashboard, not a
settings screen: the person's day, the applications ready to use, and the connectors that feed
them, all on one sheet of brand glass over their own plate.

**Layout on a 1920 frame.** One glass panel 1656 wide at 132 / 120, padding 40 48 36. Head:
the date and time as the eyebrow, a chip at the right ("One memory · 7 connected"). Headline
56px: "Three things need you today." Two columns, 1fr and 600px, gap 44.

Left, **Today**: hairline rows of time, the line, the state, with the mail row first and
explicit: "Marta's mail: read, filed, the reply drafted in your voice."

Right, **Ready to use**: a two-by-two of application tiles at `radius-card`, white at 55
percent with a 70 percent white rim, each with a live dot, the name at 20px, a state label in
blue caps at the corner and one line of status at 16px. The four in the film: Circle ("Lisbon,
four of you. The itinerary is with everyone."), Trip ("Nine days. Itinerary drafted, hotel held."), Health
("Check-up on the 14th, booked. Three runs this week."), Learning ("Portuguese, day 12. Ten
minutes, when you like."). Money is the fifth application on the rail.

Bottom, **Connected**: a pill per connector with a toggle, 34 by 20, blue on, knob white, then
"Nothing to set up" at the right. In the product and the product film each pill carries the
connected product's real mark. On anything public (the site, social, decks) the pills name the
kind of thing instead (Mail, Calendar, Files, Bank) with a neutral square, never a vendor.

**The rail** across the top names the applications: Today · Circle · Trip · Health · Learning ·
Money. Each is a space of its own with the same glass and the same hairline table. "Application"
is the public word, "space" the internal one: one brain, one door, spaces cut by what people
share, never by data source. Launch with life admin, money, trips and the circle, then one
space a month. Sharing a space into a circle is one checkbox, not a per-fact chore.

**On film** the dashboard is scanned, not shown: the macro register travels from the headline
across the rows to the tiles, a cursor lands on a tile, one tile lifts, then the camera settles
on the connector row. The message to land: the complexity is gone, everything is ready.

**Never.** A settings look, no forms and no labels-and-inputs; more than one primary action; a
tile without a status; a vendor name on a public surface; a toggle in any colour but the one
blue.
